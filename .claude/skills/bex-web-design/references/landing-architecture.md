# Landing architecture and content completeness

Read for new pages, substantive redesigns, section planning, or completeness reviews. This is a decision framework, not a universal funnel, fixed section count, or permission to expand a narrow edit. Source IDs resolve in [research sources](research-sources.md). Recommended orders below are design synthesis; their exact order and conversion impact are not experimentally established.

## Choose the page role before the business recipe

- **Campaign landing:** one audience/offer and a dominant conversion goal matching the incoming ad, email, or search intent. Repeat the same useful action at relevant decision points; keep supporting navigation and policy access when needed. Do not automatically reproduce a large homepage menu. [L1]
- **Squeeze / opt-in page:** a focused contact exchange for a real resource, subscription or access request. State the deliverable, who it helps, necessary fields and what happens after submission. Minimize competing tasks while retaining appropriate privacy/contact routes and an ordinary exit. Do not turn a ready-to-buy or booking journey into compulsory lead capture. [F6]
- **Splash / entry gate:** an intermediate decision before the destination, not a campaign page or required cinematic intro. Add only for a documented entry need or explicitly chosen brand experience; a hero or notice may serve the purpose with less interruption. Define entry trigger, actual choice, return-visit behavior and destination. Optional intros need immediate skip/continue; preserve deep links and browser Back. Do not impose geolocation redirects or infer an age-verification obligation from a showcase. [F10]
- **Homepage:** explain who the business serves and what it offers, then expose meaningful routes across its products/services and audience needs. Preserve navigation and breadth; do not force every visitor through one sales funnel. [U1]
- **Product detail:** put representative media, actual options, price, availability, and purchase action together; surface applicable delivery/return conditions near the buying decision. Follow with use, specifications, fit, and evidence. A cinematic story cannot replace the purchase information. [U3]
- **Other task/content surfaces:** retain the actual purpose and existing architecture. Do not turn an article, directory, or account page into a landing page just because it uses this skill.

For the selected role, extract from the user's supplied research/brief: business/offer, audience, traffic promise, what the visitor already knows, primary action and its commitment, decision-critical facts, available evidence, and scope (study, plan, copy draft, full page, or revision). Follow the [research intake](workflow.md#1-receive-business-and-audience-research); distinguish supplied findings, stakeholder beliefs, and unknowns. Make design inferences explicit, and ask only for unresolved information that materially changes the result. Do not invent audience/market research. A campaign for a B2B ebook is a resource-download page, not automatically an enterprise-demo page.

For a multi-page marketing site, first map each page's purpose, navigation, reusable components, and the route to the main action. Then apply the section framework to the relevant pages. A single campaign landing page needs its decision path, not an obligatory multi-page sitemap.

For substantive navigation decisions use [navigation architecture and behavior](navigation.md). Page role determines whether broad exploration, focused opt-in or an entry choice is appropriate; neither a missing navbar nor a single button guarantees a useful journey.

## Map coverage, then order it

Read the saved [Brand, UX and Creative Design Brief](brand-document-synthesis.md) for supplied audience findings, decision questions, proposition, proof, and narrative opportunities. Give the site a coherent story through those useful answers; express the promise through the sequence rather than repeating its slogan or adding a mandatory brand-history section. Keep inferred journey choices distinct from documented user behavior.

Make a compact section map before detailed choreography:

| Visitor question | Answer and source / missing fact | Section and priority | Why here | Action or continuation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What is this and is it for me? | Actual offer and intended audience | Opening | Establish relevance | Real primary next step |
| What will I get, and why believe it? | Demonstration, scope, evidence | Relevant benefit/detail sections | Support the specific claim | Inspect, choose, or continue |
| What does it cost or require? | Price/conditions or known pricing basis | Near evaluation/action | Resolve commitment | Compare or proceed |

These are coverage prompts, not three mandatory sections. Merge related answers, repeat a crucial condition where useful, and split only when it improves comprehension. No minimum section count. Required information with no source is a named content gap, not a reason to generate a testimonial, guarantee, statistic, or price. Missing optional proof may be omitted; missing essential decision information means the complete page is not release-ready.

## Business starting points

Choose the relevant row and adapt it to the actual intent. Each sequence includes only supported material; proof may be integrated beside claims. The opening includes a clear offer and reachable primary action unless the requested surface has a different purpose.

| Business / goal | Suggested section sequence | Conditions that change the structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DTC / consumer-product campaign | Offer and product-in-use -> benefits/demonstration -> product or bundle choice and price -> material details -> authentic evidence -> purchase conditions/remaining questions -> final action | For ready-to-buy traffic, bring options, price, and key delivery terms into the opening. Weight, size, fit, ingredients, or compatibility depend on the product. Do not hide them all in FAQ. [L2, U3] |
| Self-serve SaaS | Outcome and real product preview -> brief relevant proof -> key use cases/how it works -> benefits with supporting capabilities -> plans/trial terms -> adoption obstacles -> signup | Integrations, migration, security, comparison, and cancellation details appear when decision-critical. Never infer a free trial, no-card requirement, or savings figure. |
| Enterprise / high-consideration B2B | Business outcome and fit -> relevant evidence -> solution/use case -> case study -> integration, implementation and support -> cost basis -> what a demo/inquiry entails -> form | Let evaluators inspect useful information before surrendering contact details. Use only actual certifications/results. Exact price may be unavailable; show supplied scenarios or pricing factors without fabricating ranges. [L3] |
| Agency / architecture / professional service | Specialism and intended client -> selected relevant work -> services/deliverables -> collaboration process -> responsible people/credibility -> project scope, time and cost basis -> inquiry | Productized fixed-price service may show packages much earlier. A young studio can show honestly labeled concept work or methods; not fictional clients or completed projects. |
| Local / appointment service | Service, location/coverage and quick action -> options, cost and duration -> expertise/evidence -> visit/work process -> availability or estimate request -> cancellation conditions -> contact/access | Show location/hours early if they determine eligibility. Use direct booking only when actual scheduling supports it; bespoke work may need an estimate instead. [L4] |
| Course / training | Learning outcome and intended learner -> level/prerequisites -> curriculum/sample -> instructor/evidence -> delivery, access and support -> price/terms -> remaining questions and enrollment | Distinguish live dates/timezone from self-paced access. Do not promise employment, credentials, or lifetime access without evidence. [L5] |
| Event / webinar | Topic, date/timezone, place or online format and registration -> reason to attend -> program -> speakers/performers -> ticket choices/inclusions -> attendance logistics/terms -> registration | Time/location are opening information, not a footer surprise. Capacity, recordings, deadlines, and urgency must be real. [L6] |
| Resource download / newsletter / waitlist | Concrete value, audience and action/form -> contents/sample -> relevance and author credibility -> delivery/contact expectations | Often compact. Do not import pricing grids, team stories, or long FAQs without a need. Waitlist signup must not imply purchase, access, or a reserved appointment. [L1] |

## Hierarchy adjustments

- **Unfamiliar visitor:** explain the category/problem and demonstrate the solution early; avoid invented fears. **Comparing visitor:** prioritize differences, fit, evidence, limits, and cost. **Ready visitor:** prioritize the actual offer, terms, and action. These are useful intent hypotheses, not mandatory linear psychological stages. [L7]
- Put proof beside the claim it supports. An unrelated logo wall does not validate a performance claim. Give case studies context, actual work, and supported outcomes.
- FAQ answers remaining questions; it is not the sole home of essential terms. About/founder story, comparisons, metrics, partner logos, testimonials, and guarantees are conditional, not fillers.
- Preserve contact, support, and applicable policy routes in appropriate header/footer locations. Do not fabricate legal policies, shipping promises, or available destinations to fill a footer.
- Headings alone should outline a coherent offer and decision path. Use semantic nesting and meaningful groupings, not an all-H2 visual-size convention. [L8]
- Map motion onto the chosen content: signature opening, useful transformations, readable detail sections, then a reachable action. Not every section needs a pin, new effect, or full viewport. Keep necessary information and controls accessible on mobile, with reduced motion, and when scenes are skipped.

## Resolve the promise through the complete action

Check **incoming promise → opening explanation → relevant demonstration/proof → decision conditions → actual action → confirmation/delivery**. Use the supplied campaign language and audience evidence; do not generate new research or invent reassurance. The opening should communicate relevance without waiting for a motion sequence. Place supported answers to likely objections where the decision occurs. [F7, F9]

Choose form fields by their operational purpose; a short form is useful only if it still enables the promised service. Keep required costs, eligibility and obligations visible before commitment. Do not postpone security steps, disguise subscriptions or manufacture urgency to reduce apparent friction. For an opt-in, distinguish request received from resource delivered; confirm only the state the integration actually reached. [F6, F8]

Treat choices of headline, visual, CTA placement and form length as reasoned design hypotheses. A construction review or alternative sketch cannot establish a conversion winner. The supplied A/B article was reviewed for this evidence boundary; do not add experiments, analytics, event instrumentation or outcome measurement to this owner's production workflow. [F4]

## Content-completeness gate

For a requested complete page, inspect the actual delivered content against the map:

1. The page role, offer, intended visitor, and next step are understandable.
2. Each decision-critical question has a discoverable supported answer, or a clearly reported release-blocking gap. A justified link to real deeper detail can suffice; do not bury essential terms behind signup.
3. Selected sections contain usable copy and relevant content, not just section names or empty cards. Unnecessary sections are omitted or merged deliberately.
4. Labels, forms, and destinations match the real action, with the relevant conditions before commitment. For implementation, follow the action through its observed result; a copy draft cannot certify runtime behavior.
5. The journey is complete beyond the signature scene, including appropriate contact/policy access; reading order and key information survive mobile and motion alternatives.

Record content completeness separately from functionality, cinematic craft, and client acceptance. A motion study may be delivered when that is the requested scope, but it must stay labeled a study. For a full build, map the content and visual direction, then build an early representative slice that resolves the largest implementation uncertainty; this is often the signature scene. Continue through the complete requested site without creating an unsolicited approval checkpoint. This is construction, not a user-testing phase. Do not claim unmeasured conversion gains or append measurement work to this production workflow.
