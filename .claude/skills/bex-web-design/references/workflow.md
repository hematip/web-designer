# AI website production workflow

This is an adaptation of a broader UX lifecycle for the owner's AI website-generation work. It is not a claim that research, user testing, or business measurement are unnecessary in other contexts. Here, **business and audience research come from the user**, and the workflow ends with delivery or authorized publication. Do not insert usability studies, participant testing, A/B testing, analytics setup, business outcome measurement, or ongoing optimization. Artifact inspection and required software checks remain inside construction.

The seven steps describe dependencies, not a rigid waterfall, seven mandatory documents, or seven approval rounds. Revisit an earlier decision when actual copy, assets, or implementation exposes a concrete problem. Read specialist references only as they become useful. At each major stage consult the saved analysis brief's summary and relevant sections; trace important decisions to their findings and source locators. On new input, update that brief and revisit only affected decisions. Keep the analysis internal to the project/handoff, outside the public site.

## Scope the route

- **New site or substantial redesign:** follow the seven steps below, reusing decisions already established by the user and approved materials.
- **Brief/concept only:** complete the requested planning or direction work and stop at that deliverable.
- **Small copy, type, layout, or motion revision:** inspect the relevant baseline and source, make the scoped change, check affected states, and deliver. Do not reopen business intake or redesign accepted regions.
- **Review only:** inspect and report; repair only when requested. A deployment request uses the existing intended version and release checks.

## 1. Receive business and audience research

Read the conversation, supplied documents, and user-designated sources before asking questions. The input may be brand books, identity/strategy documents, audience/product information, a research report, approved brief, interview summary, or the user's informal explanation. Follow [document synthesis](brand-document-synthesis.md) for reading coverage, rendered visual inspection, source authority, and evidence types. These files feed UX analysis, voice/copy, storytelling, and creative direction, not just palette and logo extraction. Existing material counts; do not ask them to repeat it or complete a long form.

| Input | Extract or ask only when relevant and missing |
| --- | --- |
| Business and offer | What is being offered, to whom, why it is useful, supported differentiation, material terms/constraints, and the intended visitor action |
| Audience | Priority group, task/problem, context and awareness, questions or objections, decision information, and language/access needs known to the user |
| Evidence | Which statements are research findings, stakeholder beliefs, approved business facts, or unresolved assumptions; their supplied sources |
| Delivery | Landing page or multi-page scope, languages, brand/assets, existing site/system, real action destinations/integrations, and material time/technical constraints |

Apply the [product-design coverage](brand-document-synthesis.md#product-design-coverage-in-research-and-define) proportionately: prioritize audience roles and tasks, understand entry/context and decision criteria, trust and content needs, language/access requirements, operational handoffs, and implementation constraints. Document how relevant findings change architecture, copy, hierarchy, interactions, and narrative pacing. Demographic descriptions alone do not settle these decisions; do not invent missing behavioral evidence.

Treat unknowns honestly. Do not manufacture a persona, user quote, pain point, market fact, competitor conclusion, or research finding. A stakeholder belief is not participant evidence. The skill synthesizes supplied research; it does not automatically undertake primary research or independent market research.

If the offer, primary action, or audience need is too unclear to make consequential design choices, ask the smallest useful bundled question. Continue independent project/asset inspection while awaiting the answer. Nonessential gaps can remain explicit; the absence of a formal research report is not a blocker when the user has provided a sufficient explanation. A hypothetical brief requires explicit user authorization and stays labeled as such.

**Working result:** a saved Brand, UX and Creative Design Brief, scaled to the inputs, with source coverage and locators, audience/UX findings, brand meaning, fixed requirements, creative opportunities, and named gaps. Extend the [current project record](project-templates.md#brand-ux-and-creative-design-brief) rather than creating competing summaries. **Ready when:** the next design decisions have a basis, or their hypothetical status has been expressly authorized. This is an internal condition, not a new approval ritual.

## 2. Synthesize the brief, offer, and message

Translate supplied information into the site's purpose, priority audience/task, offer, supported differentiator, main message, actual primary action, and constraints. Separate exact approved public copy from internal strategy. Identify important claims for which evidence is missing instead of polishing them into apparent facts.

Develop the same brief into a usable experience narrative and voice contract. Distinguish source findings from proposed creative interpretation. Translate the proposition and slogan into opportunities for copy, composition, interactions, and narrative scroll; retain freedom where the sources do not prescribe a treatment.

Use the [Define guidance](brand-document-synthesis.md#converge-into-an-actionable-define) to turn analysis into a focused problem, priority audience/task, scope and non-goals, essential requirements and dependencies, and concrete experience principles. Set observable artifact criteria to use while building, not promises about conversion or proof of comprehension.

**Working result:** the focused problem, prioritized requirements, core proposition, UX/message priorities, voice, and creative narrative in the same saved brief. **Ready when:** the AI can explain who the experience serves, what they need to understand/do, why the selected priorities matter, what the main action actually does, and which dependencies remain. Do not require a KPI baseline, outcome target, experiment plan, or analytics specification.

## 3. Map the journey and architecture

Use [landing architecture](landing-architecture.md) to match the page's role and business model. For a multi-page marketing site, determine page roles, navigation, shared components, and the path between discovery, detail, and action. For a single landing page, map the decision sequence directly.

For each necessary section map **visitor question -> sourced answer/evidence -> section/placement -> next step**. Choose and order sections by need, not a universal hero-benefits-testimonials-pricing template. Include only evidence and sections that belong to this offer. A low-detail layout sketch is useful when grouping or density is unresolved; it is not a compulsory extra deliverable.

When a shared subject could connect these answers, mark its possible roles and meaningful destination in the same map. Develop it through [continuous object storytelling](scroll-object-storytelling.md) during ideation; choose the subject from the brief and available assets. Keep the required content journey intact rather than inventing sections merely to lengthen a motion path.

**Working result:** a page/section map. **Ready when:** important visitor questions and the action path have a place, and factual gaps are visible.

## 4. Prepare content and asset direction

Use [UX copywriting](ux-copywriting.md) for headlines, explanations, benefits, supporting evidence, action labels, and interface messages. Write representative real copy before sizing layouts. Map approved assets or authorized asset creation to their role and fidelity requirements. Separate publishable copy/captions from internal provenance and production notes using the [public-content boundary](ux-copywriting.md#public-content-and-internal-production-notes); asset metadata does not become public text by default. Do not use invented proof or arbitrary filler to complete the composition.

Content preparation and structure can inform each other. A newly discovered decision-critical term can change section placement; missing final imagery may permit an isolated geometry study, but not a claim that the complete page is finished.

**Working result:** the section/copy/asset map, merged with the current record where practical. **Ready when:** the direction can be designed with realistic density, tone, imagery, and action behavior. Finalize all in-scope copy before delivery.

## 5. Resolve visual direction and cinematic language

Use [visual direction](visual-direction.md), [typography system](typography-system.md), [UX/UI design](ux-ui-design.md), and [cinematic direction](cinematic-direction.md) for the relevant decisions. Brand constraints are collected earlier; the visual treatment is resolved here against the message, structure, and real content.

Use [design diversity](design-diversity.md) for open direction choices or repetitive outputs: compare distinct premises, hero compositions and signature mechanisms on the same message, and inspect useful references when available. Do not carry over an opening frame, photographic hero or palette simply from past taste. Specify identity constraints, visual treatment, motion thesis, and implementation separately. A named style is optional. Preserve the owner's cinematic/wow default for new sites and substantial redesigns while adapting the mechanism to visitor tasks and brand. An explicit static or native-scroll request takes precedence.

Assess whether [continuous object storytelling](scroll-object-storytelling.md) would strengthen this narrative. When selected, resolve subject identity, chapter roles, path and pauses, destination, text safe areas, representation, and mobile/static equivalents in the existing storyboard. Make this choice within scope without waiting for the user to request a moving object or a particular library. Choose a different signature mechanism when continuity would obscure content or lacks a meaningful role.

Consider [text motion](text-motion.md) within the same storyboard: how exact headlines, slogans, or phrases move with the scene and develop the brand narrative. Resolve text units, progression, reading hold and responsive/static composition here; select the compatible library during implementation. A text effect is conditional, not a new required deliverable or a preset for every section.

Reuse an existing appropriate system. If a meaningful visual choice remains open, compare compact alternatives using the same content and selection criteria; do not build multiple whole websites or require another approval by default. Resolve typography with actual language specimens, not just font names.

**Working result:** a concise direction, reusable UI rules, and signature-scene storyboard. **Ready when:** typography, composition, imagery, motion, mobile adaptation, and reduced-motion treatment are concrete enough to build.

For a new visual system, use [layout and visual foundations](layout-and-visual-foundations.md) to resolve actual spacing/padding roles, content widths, grid behavior, hierarchy, icon family, and surface treatment. Use [component sourcing](component-sourcing.md) to choose compatible real primitives and assets where useful. These are part of the same direction and project record, not a new phase or approval deliverable.

Read [interface foundations](prototype-foundations.md) here when resolving a new system; load its applicable references before related decisions. Record semantic colors/theme pairs, radius and icon roles, responsive density, interaction/status transitions and functional motion alongside existing layout/type choices. Do not turn optional theme or product-prototype capabilities into new website features.

## 6. Build the complete requested site

Build a representative slice around the largest implementation uncertainty. With a settled action path, this will often be the signature scene. A complex form or constrained layout may need an earlier slice. Integrate cinematic direction early and continue through the complete requested journey; a hero or motion demo alone does not satisfy a full-site request.

Keep the existing stack for revisions. Choose the animation engine from the resolved scene: use [Motion integration](framer-motion-integration.md) for suitable React interactions/layout/scroll-linked motion or [GSAP choreography](gsap-choreography.md) for complex pinned/scrubbed sequences. Apply [Lenis integration](lenis-integration.md) when smooth scrolling is needed and [motion and assets](motion-and-assets.md) for composition. Their preferences, fidelity contracts, and fallback requirements remain in force. Use [Three.js integration](threejs-integration.md) when live spatial rendering supports the visual direction; choose it within scope without waiting for the user to name the library. Resolve assets and rendering/lifecycle ownership in this construction pass. Conditional tools are not a mandatory library bundle.

Check the artifact as it is built: real content, honest actions and states, navigation/links, asset fidelity, typography, keyboard/focus, responsive and RTL behavior, reduced motion, scene midpoints, reverse scroll, direct anchors, and existing project requirements. Scope checks to changed regions for small edits. Correct observed problems and recheck affected states. Do not replace rendering/playback inspection with a successful compilation. Technical performance inspection concerns execution quality; it is not business outcome measurement or a reason to add tracking.

When implementing text choreography, follow [text motion](text-motion.md) for core Motion versus premium Motion+, GSAP SplitText, or conditional Anime.js/SplitType integration. Verify exact copy, script shaping, semantic access, font/width reflow, cleanup, and a readable dependency-failure/reduced-motion state within this construction pass.

When implementing a persistent subject journey, follow [continuous object storytelling](scroll-object-storytelling.md#implementation-contract) for scene ownership, DOM-to-scene anchors, scroll-derived state, depth/scale, and destination alignment. Inspect the actual cross-section handoff early, then complete the page and verify reverse/skip/anchor entry, resize, and simplified modes. Keep this inside construction rather than adding another production phase.

**Working result:** the complete implementation and proportionate evidence of observed quality. **Ready when:** content, copy, typography, functional behavior, and cinematic craft each meet the requested brief, or actual limitations are clearly disclosed. A passing function does not prove creative acceptance; the client's opinion does not prove participant research findings.

Apply the [foundation quality gate](layout-and-visual-foundations.md#foundation-quality-gate) to the representative first slice and the completed affected layout. Correct shared rules before repeating components. Verify sourced components still behave correctly after theming; no requirement to use a particular vendor, framework, or ready-made page template is implied.

## 7. Deliver or publish within scope

Use [review and release](review-and-release.md). Deliver usable files or an authorized, verified live destination. Report what changed, the important observed checks, and real limitations. Clearly identify demonstration-only interactions, missing integrations, or incomplete dependencies. Do not present a simulated submit as a completed booking or lead submission.

**Working result:** the requested site/direction/revision and a compact handoff. **End here:** no automatic analytics implementation, measurement plan, post-launch optimization phase, or monitoring task. Do not claim conversion uplift or verified user comprehension without evidence.

## Keep the process lightweight

- Maintain one evolving record using only useful fields from [project templates](project-templates.md); no mandatory bundle of phase documents.
- Reuse supplied research, existing design decisions, source assets, and the current stack. Ask only unresolved consequential questions.
- Use internal readiness criteria to advance autonomously within scope. Request input when necessary; do not introduce routine approval ceremonies.
- A discovery in copy or construction sends work back only to the relevant decision. It does not restart the whole process.
- Keep the specialist creative and implementation standards. Efficiency comes from less redundant work, not weaker content, typography, motion, or responsive quality.

This sequencing is a practical synthesis. The workflow exclusions and user-owned research intake are explicit owner requirements; the [research context](research-sources.md#ai-production-workflow-adaptation) does not establish them as universal UX rules.
