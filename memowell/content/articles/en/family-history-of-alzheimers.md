Watching a parent or a brother or sister live with Alzheimer's disease changes how you think about your own memory. Every lost name becomes a question. This article is about what the risk figures actually mean, so that the question can be answered calmly.

## What "first-degree relative" means

A first-degree relative is a parent, a sibling or a child. If one of them has Alzheimer's disease, your own lifetime chance of developing it is higher than that of someone with no family history: roughly double, according to the figures Kondor Pharma cites. Two things should be said straight away about that number.

First, it is a comparison between groups, not a prediction about one person. Most people with an affected parent do not develop the disease, and many people with no family history do.

Second, family history is one factor among several. Age is the largest. Cardiovascular health, blood pressure, diabetes, physical activity, sleep, hearing, education and social contact all appear in the research on risk, and several of them can be changed.

## Genes

A small number of families carry rare gene changes that cause early-onset Alzheimer's disease in middle age, passed from one generation to the next. These families usually know, because the pattern is unmistakable. For everyone else, genes make a difference of degree: some common variants raise the chance, others lower it, and none of them decides the outcome. Genetic testing is a conversation for a doctor or a genetic counsellor, not a shop.

## What to do with the information

Treat higher risk as a reason to pay attention earlier, not as a verdict.

- Talk to your doctor about your family history and ask whether a baseline memory assessment makes sense for you, so that any change later can be measured against something.
- Look after the risk factors that can be changed: blood pressure, movement, sleep, hearing, weight, smoking.
- Stay socially and mentally active. The research on these habits is observational, but it points consistently in the same direction.
- If you decide with your doctor to try Memowell®, start it under their supervision and review it together.

## If you are also the carer

Many people reading this are caring for the relative in question. Your own sleep, stress and general health matter, both for you and for the care you give. The caregivers' articles on this site, and the page on who Memowell® is for, have more on that.
