Frankincense is the dried resin of *Boswellia* trees, which grow in the dry regions of the Arabian Peninsula, the Horn of Africa and India. It has been burned, chewed and brewed for thousands of years, and in the texts of traditional medicine the resin was recommended, among other uses, for memory.

## The compounds

What makes the resin interesting to researchers is a group of molecules called boswellic acids. There are several; the one studied most is AKBA, acetyl-11-keto-beta-boswellic acid. Laboratory and animal research has examined boswellic acids for their anti-inflammatory activity, in particular their ability to inhibit an enzyme called 5-lipoxygenase, which drives one of the body's inflammatory pathways, and to a lesser extent the cyclooxygenase enzymes. Research also describes them as modulators of the immune system.

Inflammation in the nervous system is one of the processes being studied in memory disorders, which is why these compounds attracted attention. The article on inflammation in the nervous system on this site explains that side of the story.

## The problem: absorption

Boswellic acids are large, fat-loving molecules, and the digestive system absorbs very little of them. A person can take plain frankincense resin for weeks and only a small fraction of the active compounds reach the bloodstream. This is the main reason the traditional use was hard to study and hard to reproduce: dose, quality and absorption varied from one preparation to the next.

## What K-Vie™ is

K-Vie™ is Kondor Pharma's answer to that problem. ## What this does and does not tell you

Knowing how a compound works in the laboratory is not the same as knowing what it does for a person. The mechanism above explains why boswellic acids were worth studying in people; the published studies on this site are where the clinical evidence sits, and they are described in plain language on the home page and in detail in the professional section. Whether these mechanisms account for the clinical findings has not been established.
