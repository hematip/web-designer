"Brain fog" is one of those phrases that everyone understands and no textbook defines. People use it for a cloudy, slow, unfocused feeling: thoughts that will not line up, a word that will not come, a page read three times. It is real, it is common, and it is not one thing.

## Several things wearing one name

- **Lack of sleep.** The most common cause and the most overlooked. A few short nights are enough.
- **Illness and recovery.** Many infections leave a foggy period behind them, sometimes for weeks. The same is true after surgery and anaesthesia, particularly in older people.
- **Medicines.** Sleeping tablets, some antihistamines, some painkillers and several other common medicines can cloud thinking, alone or in combination. This is worth a review with a pharmacist or doctor; never stop a prescribed medicine on your own.
- **Mood and stress.** Low mood and anxiety both affect concentration and memory, and both are treatable.
- **Hormonal change.** Many women describe fog around the menopause.
- **Thyroid, blood sugar, vitamin B12 and other medical causes** that a blood test can find.

## Delirium is different

Delirium is sudden confusion: it comes on over hours or days, fluctuates through the day, and often brings disorientation, drowsiness or agitation with it. It is common in older people in hospital, after an operation or with an infection, and it is a medical emergency, not a fog to wait out. If someone becomes suddenly confused, call a doctor the same day.

## Where inflammation comes in

Research has studied inflammation in the nervous system as a factor in both brain fog and delirium. In delirium, inflammatory signals from an infection or an injury are thought to reach the brain and disturb its function; in longer-lasting fog after illness, similar processes are under investigation. This is an area of active research, not a settled answer.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor if the fog lasts more than a few weeks, if it is getting worse, if it interferes with work or daily tasks, if other people have noticed it, or if it came on suddenly. Take a note of when it started, what was happening in your life at the time, how you sleep, and every medicine and supplement you take. Most causes of brain fog can be identified, and many can be put right.
