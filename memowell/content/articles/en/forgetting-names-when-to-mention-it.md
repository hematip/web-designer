Forgetting a name is one of the most common complaints in any waiting room, and most of the time it means nothing. The name of an actor, a neighbour's grandchild, a restaurant visited once: these come and go all through adult life. Tiredness, stress, a bad night and simply having too much on one's mind all make it worse.

So the question is not whether you forget names. It is whether a pattern has changed.

## Lapses that are usually ordinary

- A name or word that is on the tip of your tongue and surfaces later.
- Forgetting why you walked into a room, then remembering.
- Misplacing keys or glasses and finding them by retracing your steps.
- Needing a moment longer to learn a new phone or a new route.
- Forgetting the name of someone you rarely see.

## Patterns worth mentioning

- Forgetting the names of people you see often, or of close family members.
- Asking the same question several times in one conversation without realising it.
- Losing the thread of a conversation or a film, and not being able to pick it up again.
- Getting lost on a familiar route, or being unsure of the day or the season.
- Difficulty following a recipe, paying a bill or planning something with several steps.
- Changes other people notice before you do: a spouse, a child, a colleague.
- A change in mood or personality that goes along with the forgetting.

None of these is a diagnosis. Each is a reason to talk to a doctor, because the earlier a change is looked at, the more options there are, and because some causes of memory change are straightforward to put right.

## What the doctor will do

Expect questions about what you have noticed and since when; a short memory test; a blood test to rule out common causes such as thyroid or vitamin problems; and a look at your medicines, since some can cloud memory. The doctor may ask to see you again in a few months to compare.

## Before the visit

Write down three things: what you have noticed, roughly when it started, and every medicine and supplement you take. Take someone who knows you well; they will remember details you might not.
