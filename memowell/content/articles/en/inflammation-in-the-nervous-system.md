Inflammation is the body's response to injury or threat. In the brain, researchers have found that it plays a part in many conditions of the nervous system, and that it is one of the factors behind forgetfulness and a decline in memory.

In Alzheimer's disease, inflammatory signals are linked to a build-up of two proteins in the brain, amyloid-beta and tau. These deposits are among the clearest signs of age-related memory diseases. The enzymes that produce them, such as beta-secretase and certain kinases, are switched on by inflammatory molecules. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a chemical reaction in a cell.

Boswellic acids, the compounds in the resin of *Boswellia* trees, have been studied for their ability to inhibit two inflammatory enzymes in the brain, lipoxygenase and cyclooxygenase. Research on these compounds also describes them as modulators of the immune system, which has its own part in controlling inflammation.
