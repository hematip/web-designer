## Part 1. Client brief (summarised from the client's answers)

### Purpose
- The site is for **branding and awareness** of Memowell.
- **Problem it solves:** some people notice small memory changes in themselves or someone close to them, or worry about Alzheimer's. Today they cannot find clear, reliable information about memory, Alzheimer's and this product in one place.
- **Goals, in priority order:**
  1. Introduce Memowell: what it is, who it is for, how and when it is taken, and when it must not be taken.
  2. Raise awareness about memory and Alzheimer's in plain, calm language, without frightening anyone.
  3. Present the study data so a non-specialist understands it and a specialist can reach the source (DOI).
  4. Build credibility and familiarity, so visitors return later with a question or to buy.
- **What the site does NOT do:**
  - It does not sell directly.
  - It is not only a route to purchase.
  - It does not give medical advice.
- **Principles the client asked for:**
  - Every claim and every number has a source.
  - The tone is empathetic and calm, because the subject is sensitive.
  - The catalogue's "under the supervision of a physician" is repeated on the site.
  - **Readability for older people and their caregivers is a priority.**

### Audiences, in priority order
There is **no separate entry per audience** (no "I am a patient / I am a doctor" gateway). This is the client's decision.

1. **Patients and people at risk.**
   - Who: people with Alzheimer's, mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or brain fog; people after a brain injury; people with a first-degree relative with Alzheimer's; people in mid-life approaching 65.
   - They want to understand what Memowell is and who it is for, in simple language, with directions and contraindications made clear.
   - Some are elderly or have memory problems.
2. **Caregivers** (family or nurse).
   - They want information they can use to decide on behalf of, or together with, the patient:
     - directions
     - cautions
     - study results
     - whether it can be taken with the patient's current medicines
   - They may also want information for themselves.
3. **Healthcare professionals** (doctors, pharmacists, dietitians).
   - They want precise, sourced data (DOI):
     - results tables
     - composition and dose
     - contraindications and interactions
4. **Distributors** (pharmacies, sales partners).
   - They want:
     - brand and product information
     - specifications and packaging
     - the catalogue
     - the countries where it is sold
     - a way to contact the company about partnership
   - Their path differs from the other three: they want business, not awareness.

### Visitor situation
- The main visitor is either worried about their own memory or concentration, or is a family member searching on someone's behalf.
- Today they may be on prescribed medication, another memory supplement or ordinary frankincense, a diet or lifestyle change, or nothing yet.
- The site must explain:
  - **how Memowell differs from ordinary frankincense and other supplements**
  - where it sits alongside current care
- Familiarity varies:
  - Some visitors have only just noticed a change.
  - Others have a diagnosis or are caregivers who want depth.
- So the main content is simple, and deeper scientific information is available for those who want it.

### How visitors arrive
- QR code on the printed catalogue
- social media
- Amazon
- Google search for keywords such as "memory" and "memory supplement"
- referral by a doctor or pharmacy

### Questions visitors have before deciding

## Part 2. Strategic decisions already made (approved IA)

1. **Primary visitor action** = *a conversation with a doctor or pharmacist*, not a purchase.
   - The site prepares visitors for that conversation with a downloadable one-page summary:
     - composition
     - directions
     - studies
     - five questions to ask
   - It can be measured through downloads and contact.
2. **Professional content** lives on this same site, in a clearly labelled section, with **no login**.
   - Its entrance carries a non-blocking note: "This section is written for healthcare professionals…"
3. **No audience gateway.**
   - The homepage is written in plain language for patients and caregivers.
   - Professionals reach their section through a professional band and the footer.
   - Distributors reach theirs through the footer, the homepage and the About submenu.
4. **The professional band repeats at the end of every content page**, not only on the homepage.
5. The nav label must read exactly **"For healthcare professionals"**, never "HCP" or "Specialist".
6. **Patients and caregivers share one path.** There is no separate caregiver page. The same pages carry the deeper layer, for example interactions and cautions.
7. **Case reports** appear only in the professional section, behind a button, with the two-sentence warning from the copy.
8. **Testimonials:** none in v1.
   - Reserve a place for real feedback later, gathered from the feedback form or Amazon reviews, with permission.
   - Do not publish it in v1.

## Part 4. Approved English copy: use verbatim

Rules:
- Text in the blocks below goes on the site **exactly as written**.
- `[Square brackets]` are button or link labels.
- Lines beginning with *Note:* are editor notes for you. **Do not publish them.**

### Writing rules the copy follows
Keep to these if anything has to be adjusted, and flag every adjustment:
- Every number appears with its study population and source.
- Study results are written in the past tense ("scored higher", not "improves").
- The product name is always **Memowell®**: with ® on first mention per page, plain afterwards. Never ™.
- The product is a **supplement**, never a drug or medication.
- Every content page ends by inviting a conversation with a doctor or pharmacist.
- No comparison with any other brand, explicit or implied.
- **Banned words on every page:** prevents · stops · halts progression · cures · treats · no side effects · no interactions · FDA approved · guaranteed · drug · medication · better than
- **Tone:**
  - Calm and simple, never frightening.
  - Short sentences.
  - No jargon in public sections, because some readers are elderly or have memory problems.
  - Technical vocabulary appears only on `/science/professionals`.

### SCIENCE `/science`
Meta title: `What the research on Memowell® found | Memowell`
Meta description: `Three published studies, in plain language: an 85-patient trial in Alzheimer's disease, blood markers, and 80 people after brain injury. With sources.`

**Introduction**
> # What the research found
>
> Three published studies sit behind Memowell®. Here they are in plain language, each with the number of people, how long it ran, and where to read it. Nothing on this page is a promise about what will happen to you.

**Study 1**
> ## 85 people with Alzheimer's disease, six months
>
> Published in *Alzheimer's & Dementia*, 2023.
>
> Eighty-five people with Alzheimer's disease were divided into two groups. One group took Memowell alongside their prescribed care; the other took a placebo — a capsule with no active ingredient. Neither the participants nor the researchers knew who was in which group.
>
> After six months, the Memowell group scored **3.11 points higher on MMSE** than the placebo group. MMSE is a standard test of memory and orientation; a higher score means better performance.
>
> On CDR-SOB, a second standard measure, the difference was **1.65 points**, in the direction of slower change.
>
> Both differences were statistically significant (p < 0.0001), meaning they were unlikely to be chance.
>
> Source: DOI 10.1002/alz.063660

**Study 2**
> ## What changed in the blood
>
> The same research also looked at what could be measured in participants' blood.
>
> The ratio of two proteins, Aβ42 to Aβ40, rose. A falling ratio is one of the signals researchers track as Alzheimer's disease advances.
>
> More than five inflammatory compounds decreased, and the proportion of lymphocytes — a type of white blood cell — rose.
>
> Sources: DOI 10.3233/JAD-221026 · DOI 10.14283/jpad.2018.41

**Study 3**
> ## 80 people with cognitive difficulty after a brain injury
>
> Published in *Brain Injury*.
>
> Eighty people with cognitive difficulty following traumatic brain injury were studied. Scores on RAVLT — a test of verbal learning and memory — improved.
>
> Source: DOI 10.1080/02699052.2022.2059816

**Closing**
> ## Reading this honestly
>
> These are published findings in defined groups of people over defined periods. They are not a guarantee, and they do not replace what your own doctor knows about your situation.
>
> Looking for study designs, full results tables and composition? [For healthcare professionals]
>
> [Download a one-page summary (PDF)]
