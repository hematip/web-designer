Taking this to your doctor

One page, with what is in Memowell, how it is taken, the published studies, and five questions worth asking.

What is in it

The active compounds are boswellic acids, drawn from the resin of Boswellia trees — the tree that produces frankincense.

Ordinary frankincense resin contains these compounds, but the digestive system absorbs very little of them. Kondor Pharma processes the resin into K-Vie™: standardised to 40% boswellic acids, then micronized and microcrystalline so that more of the compound reaches the bloodstream.

The capsules are vegetarian and gluten-free.

How to take it

  1. One capsule with each of the three main meals. Taking it with food improves absorption.
  2. Allow eight weeks. Effects on memory symptoms are generally observed after about eight weeks of regular use.
  3. Keep taking it under supervision. For memory health, the supplement is intended for long-term, regular use with your doctor's oversight — not a short course.

Follow the directions on the packaging approved in the country where you bought it. Where they differ from this page, the packaging is correct.

What the research found

85 people with Alzheimer's disease, six months

Published in Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2023.

After six months, the Memowell group scored 3.11 points higher on MMSE than the placebo group. MMSE is a standard test of memory and orientation; a higher score means better performance.

On CDR-SOB, a second standard measure, the difference was 1.65 points, in the direction of slower change.

Source: DOI 10.1002/alz.063660

What changed in the blood

The ratio of two proteins, Aβ42 to Aβ40, rose. A falling ratio is one of the signals researchers track as Alzheimer's disease advances.

More than five inflammatory compounds decreased, and the proportion of lymphocytes — a type of white blood cell — rose.

Sources: DOI 10.3233/JAD-221026 · DOI 10.14283/jpad.2018.41

80 people with cognitive difficulty after a brain injury

Published in Brain Injury.

Eighty people with cognitive difficulty following traumatic brain injury were studied. Scores on RAVLT — a test of verbal learning and memory — improved.

Source: DOI 10.1080/02699052.2022.2059816
