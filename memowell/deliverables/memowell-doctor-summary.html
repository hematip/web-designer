What's inside Memowell®

Memowell® combines K-Vie™ boswellic acids with bacosides from Bacopa and vitamin D3.

Each capsule contains 333 mg of K-Vie™.

The boswellic acids are drawn from the resin of Boswellia trees — the tree that produces frankincense. Boswellic acids have been studied for their anti-inflammatory activity.

Ordinary frankincense resin contains these compounds, but the digestive system absorbs very little of them. Drawing on current knowledge of preparation, standardisation and formulation, Kondor Pharma developed K-Vie™ to carry the maximum amount of boswellic acids: the resin is standardised to 40% boswellic acids, then micronized and microcrystalline so that more of the compound reaches the bloodstream.

How to take it

  1. One capsule after each of the three main meals. Morning, noon and evening. Taking it with food improves absorption.
  2. Allow eight weeks. Effects on memory symptoms are generally observed after about eight weeks of regular use.
  3. Keep taking it under supervision. For memory health, the supplement is intended for long-term, regular use with your doctor's oversight — not a short course. Kondor Pharma recommends a course of at least 24 weeks.

Follow the directions on the packaging approved in the country where you bought it. Where they differ from this page, the packaging is correct.

Before you start

Take Memowell® under a doctor's supervision, and take particular care during pregnancy or breastfeeding.

What the research found

85 people with mild to moderate Alzheimer's, six months

Published in Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2023.

After six months, the Memowell® group scored 3.11 points higher on MMSE than the placebo group. MMSE is a standard test of memory and orientation; a higher score means better performance.

On CDR-SOB, a second standard measure, the difference was 1.65 points, in the direction of slower change.

Source: DOI 10.1002/alz.063660

What changed in the blood

The ratio of two proteins, Aβ42 to Aβ40, rose. A falling ratio is one of the signals researchers track as Alzheimer's disease advances.

More than five inflammatory compounds decreased, and the proportion of lymphocytes — a type of white blood cell — rose.

Sources: DOI 10.3233/JAD-221026 · DOI 10.14283/jpad.2018.41

80 people with cognitive difficulty after a brain injury

Published in Brain Injury.

Eighty people with cognitive difficulty following traumatic brain injury were studied. Scores on RAVLT — a test of verbal learning and memory — improved.

Source: DOI 10.1080/02699052.2022.2059816
